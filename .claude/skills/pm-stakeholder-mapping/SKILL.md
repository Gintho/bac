# Stakeholder Mapping

## Purpose
Prioritize stakeholders and establish engagement strategies using two complementary grids. Reveals misalignments between who holds power and who experiences consequences — a critical gap most PM stakeholder analyses miss.

**When to use:** After initial stakeholder identification, before building engagement plans. Run before major product decisions, launches, or strategy presentations.

## Key Concepts

**Grid 1 — Power × Interest:**
| | Low Interest | High Interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High Power** | Keep Satisfied | Manage Closely |
| **Low Power** | Monitor | Keep Informed |

**Grid 2 — Impact × Power:**
| | Low Power | High Power |
|---|---|---|
| **High Impact** | **Elevate Deliberately** ← critical | Manage Closely |
| **Low Impact** | Minimal engagement | Gatekeepers |

**The critical insight:** Q1 in Grid 2 (High Impact / Low Power) identifies stakeholders who face real consequences from your product decisions but lack organizational voice. These are systematically under-engaged and represent your highest-risk relationship gaps.

## Application

### Step 1: List All Stakeholders

Use a simple roster — name, role, department, relationship to product (user, buyer, influencer, decision-maker, impacted party).

### Step 2: Plot Grid 1 — Power × Interest

For each stakeholder, rate independently:
- **Power (1-5):** Ability to approve, block, or significantly influence product decisions
- **Interest (1-5):** Current engagement level and stake in the product outcome

Place in appropriate quadrant. Assign engagement strategy:
- **Manage closely:** Co-design sessions, weekly syncs, advance preview of decisions
- **Keep satisfied:** Executive briefings, milestone updates, escalation path
- **Keep informed:** Newsletter, demo days, async updates
- **Monitor:** Quarterly touchpoints, awareness only

### Step 3: Plot Grid 2 — Impact × Power

Re-assess each stakeholder independently:
- **Impact (1-5):** Degree to which your product affects their work, outcomes, or daily life
- **Power:** Same as Grid 1

### Step 4: Compare Grids — Find the Gaps

Cross-reference placements. Flag mismatches:
- Stakeholder in "Keep Informed" (Grid 1) AND "High Impact / Low Power" (Grid 2) → **Under-engaged. Elevate them.**
- Stakeholder in "Manage Closely" (Grid 1) but "Low Impact / High Power" (Grid 2) → **Gatekeeper. Manage relationship, not just decisions.**

**Gap analysis output:**
```
Name | Grid 1 Quadrant | Grid 2 Quadrant | Flag | Action
-----|-----------------|-----------------|------|-------
[Name] | Keep Informed | High Impact/Low Power | ⚠️ Under-engaged | Elevate to co-design
```

### Step 5: Plan Quadrant Migrations

For flagged stakeholders, design concrete "migration" actions:
- **Elevating** a high-impact stakeholder: Add them to discovery interviews, include in roadmap reviews, create a feedback channel
- **Activating** a low-interest high-power stakeholder: Connect product to their personal OKRs, schedule 1:1 briefings before group meetings

### Step 6: Feed Into Engagement Planning

Pass high-priority relationships to the stakeholder-engagement-advisor for detailed message framing, medium selection, cadence, and next-action planning.

## Examples

**Product: New onboarding flow redesign**

Grid 1 analysis flags Head of Sales (High Power / High Interest) as Manage Closely — obvious.

Grid 2 analysis reveals Customer Success Associates (High Impact / Low Power) — they run the onboarding calls affected by the new flow. Grid 1 had them as "Monitor."

Gap: CSAs face the biggest daily consequence of this decision but have no voice in it. Action: Add 3 CSAs to the design review process and create a feedback loop post-launch.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Running only one grid:** Grid 1 alone optimizes for political management. Grid 2 alone produces equity analysis without action. You need both.
- **Rating stakeholders with your team:** Do Grid 1 and Grid 2 assessments independently before comparing — group ratings converge on the loudest voice.
- **Treating the map as static:** Stakeholder power and interest shift. Remap at major project milestones.
- **Skipping the gap analysis (Step 4):** Two grids without comparison is just two lists. The insight lives in the delta.
